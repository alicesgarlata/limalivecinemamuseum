Kennedy Park

Introduction

In the heart of one of Lima’s most dynamic districts lies Kennedy Park, a space that combines history, urban culture, and everyday life. This park is not only a meeting point, but also a symbol of the modern identity of the Peruvian capital. Surrounded by restaurants, shops, street art, and constant movement, it has become one of the city’s most representative locations, where the traditional and the contemporary coexist naturally. Its vibrant atmosphere makes it an ideal setting for both social life and audiovisual storytelling.

General Information and Location

Kennedy Park is part of the so-called Central Park of Miraflores, located in the district of Miraflores, in Lima, Peru. It is specifically located between Larco and Diagonal Avenues, very close to the Miraflores Oval and the Virgen Milagrosa Church. [1]

It covers an approximate area of 22,000 m² and is divided into two main sections: John F. Kennedy Park and 7 de Junio Park. [2]

This space functions as the district’s main square and is considered one of the most visited points by both residents and tourists. Its strategic location connects it directly with commercial, gastronomic, and tourist areas, such as the Miraflores boardwalk and the well-known “Pizza Street.” [3]

In addition, the park hosts cultural spaces such as the Chabuca Granda Amphitheater, artisan fairs, and large public events, consolidating it as an active center of social and cultural interaction.

History of the Place

The origin of Kennedy Park dates back to the early 20th century, when the area was known as the Central Park of Miraflores. In its early stages, it was a simple urban space that gradually gained importance as the district grew and became more urbanized. [4]

Its development was influenced by relevant historical events, such as the Battle of Miraflores during the War of the Pacific in 1881, which deeply shaped the identity of the district. [5]

Over time, the park underwent several renovations. One of the most important took place in the 1990s, when the urban space was completely redesigned, incorporating pedestrian areas, gardens, and cultural spaces. [2]

The name “Kennedy Park” became popular after the installation of a bust of former U.S. President John F. Kennedy, placed in 2003 as a symbol of international friendship. [2]

Although the complex is still officially known as the Central Park of Miraflores, everyday use has consolidated the name “Kennedy” as its most recognized denomination.

Highlighted Curiosity

One of Kennedy Park’s most unique features is the presence of a large population of cats that live freely in its gardens. This phenomenon began in the 1990s, when neighbors started leaving cats in the area to control a rodent infestation. [2]

Over time, these animals became an essential part of the park’s identity. Today, they are cared for by volunteers and have become part of the tourist attraction, to the point that the place is informally known as “the park of the cats.”

This element adds a particular visual and symbolic component, reinforcing its unique character compared with other urban spaces.

Cultural and Urban Importance

Kennedy Park is not only a green space, but also a cultural node within the city. Book fairs, artistic events, musical performances, and civic activities are held there. [2]

It is also a common location for social demonstrations, national celebrations, and sports activities, positioning it as a representative stage for Lima’s public life.

Its immediate surroundings, filled with commerce, gastronomy, and nightlife, make it a hybrid space between tourism and everyday life, a characteristic that makes it especially attractive as an audiovisual location.

📍 Informational Profile – Miraflores Boardwalk

Introduction

The Miraflores Boardwalk is one of Lima’s most iconic urban spaces, where the city meets the Pacific Ocean directly. Stretching for several kilometers, this scenic corridor offers a unique combination of nature, architecture, and urban life. Its privileged location on the cliffs of the Costa Verde makes it a strategic point for both recreational activities and audiovisual production. Its wide visual perspective, natural light, and ocean horizon position it as one of the most representative locations in the Peruvian capital.

General Information and Location

The Miraflores Boardwalk is located in Lima, specifically along the coastal area of the Miraflores district. It extends approximately 5 kilometers along the cliffs bordering the Costa Verde.

It is not a single park, but a continuous set of public spaces that includes the Malecón de la Marina, Malecón Cisneros, and Malecón de la Reserva. Along its route, visitors can find emblematic parks such as Parque del Amor, Parque Antonio Raimondi, and Parque María Reiche.

The boardwalk features green areas, bike lanes, pedestrian paths, and natural viewpoints overlooking the Pacific Ocean. It is also a well-known area for paragliding, attracting both tourists and local athletes.

Its proximity to commercial centers such as Larcomar and high-end residential areas reinforces its importance as a tourist and urban axis.

History of the Place

The development of the Miraflores Boardwalk is linked to the urban expansion of the district during the 20th century. Initially, the cliff area did not have consolidated urban infrastructure and was mainly seen as a natural edge of the city.

As Lima grew and Miraflores became established as a residential and tourist district, a progressive process of urbanizing the coastal edge began. This process included the construction of parks, pedestrian paths, and public spaces designed to take advantage of the scenic value of the coastline.

During the 1980s and 1990s, the boardwalk underwent important urban interventions aimed at improving its accessibility, safety, and tourist appeal. These transformations consolidated the space as one of the city’s main recreational corridors.

Today, the boardwalk is an example of integration between the natural environment and urban design, standing out for its continuous planning and maintenance.

Highlighted Curiosity

One of the most distinctive features of the Miraflores Boardwalk is its use as a takeoff point for paragliding. Thanks to the air currents generated by the cliffs, the area is ideal for this type of aerial activity.

This makes the boardwalk one of the few urban spaces in the world where it is possible to constantly observe paragliders flying over the ocean. This activity is not only recreational, but also adds a dynamic visual element that has been used in various audiovisual productions.

In addition, Parque del Amor, located within the boardwalk circuit, stands out for its sculpture “El Beso” and its mosaics inspired by the style of Antoni Gaudí, adding a distinctive artistic component to the environment.

Cultural and Urban Importance

The Miraflores Boardwalk plays a key role as a public space for recreation, tourism, and contemplation. It is used daily by people who walk, run, cycle, or simply enjoy the ocean view.

It is also an important location for cultural events, photo sessions, and sports activities. Its open design and connection with multiple parks make it a versatile space within the city.

From an urban perspective, it represents one of the best examples of how Lima’s coastal edge has been used, integrating nature, infrastructure, and social use in a balanced way.

📍 Informational Profile – Pizza Street (San Ramón Boulevard)

Introduction

Pizza Street, officially known as San Ramón Boulevard, is one of Lima’s best-known pedestrian streets and an iconic part of the Miraflores district. For decades, it has been associated with nightlife, gastronomy, and tourism, serving as a meeting point for both locals and international visitors. Although its identity has evolved over time, the street remains a lively urban setting where restaurants, cafés, bars, and entertainment venues create a vibrant atmosphere. From an audiovisual perspective, it offers an authentic representation of Lima’s contemporary nightlife and commercial culture.

General Information and Location

Pizza Street is located in the district of Miraflores, Lima, Peru. Officially named San Ramón Boulevard (Pasaje San Ramón), it is a pedestrian street situated just a few meters from Kennedy Park and Larco Avenue, in the heart of one of the city's busiest tourist areas.

The boulevard extends for approximately two blocks and is surrounded by restaurants, cafés, bars, hotels, and commercial establishments, making it one of Miraflores' main entertainment corridors.

Pizza Street has played an important role in the commercial and social development of Miraflores. For decades, it has been one of Lima's best-known meeting places, contributing significantly to the district's tourism and hospitality industry.

📍 Informational Profile – Monumental Callao

Introduction

Monumental Callao is one of Peru’s most significant urban regeneration projects, transforming part of the historic center of Callao into a vibrant cultural and artistic district. Unlike traditional commercial or residential areas, Monumental Callao is defined by its combination of contemporary art, historic architecture, cultural events, and creative industries. Colorful murals, restored republican-era buildings, galleries, and public art installations have turned the neighborhood into a symbol of urban revitalization and cultural innovation.

General Information and Location

Monumental Callao is located in the historic center of Callao, Peru, approximately 15 kilometers west of downtown Lima and only a few minutes from the Port of Callao.

The district was developed through a public-private urban renewal initiative led by the organization Fugaz, with the objective of restoring historic buildings and promoting art, tourism, entrepreneurship, and community development.

Today, Monumental Callao is home to art galleries, cultural centers, museums, cafés, restaurants, artisan shops, design studios, and exhibition spaces. Many of the historic buildings have been carefully restored while preserving their architectural heritage.

The area has become one of Peru’s leading cultural destinations, attracting local and international visitors interested in contemporary art, photography, architecture, gastronomy, and urban culture.

History of the Place

The historic center of Callao has long been an important commercial and maritime district due to its proximity to Peru’s main seaport. During the late twentieth century, however, parts of the neighborhood experienced economic decline, urban deterioration, and reduced tourism.

In 2015, the urban regeneration project Monumental Callao was officially launched with the goal of revitalizing the historic center through culture and the arts. Historic buildings were restored, abandoned spaces were transformed into cultural venues, and local artists were invited to create large-scale murals throughout the neighborhood.

Since its opening, Monumental Callao has continued to grow, hosting exhibitions, music festivals, cultural fairs, artistic residencies, and educational programs. The project has become an international example of how art can contribute to urban renewal and social transformation.

Today, Monumental Callao is recognized as one of Peru’s most successful cultural revitalization initiatives, strengthening the identity of the historic district while encouraging sustainable tourism and creative industries.

Highlighted Curiosity

One of the most distinctive features of Monumental Callao is its extensive collection of large-scale murals painted by Peruvian and international artists. Many building façades have become open-air canvases, creating an outdoor gallery that visitors can explore on foot.

Another remarkable aspect is the restoration of historic republican-era mansions, many of which have been converted into galleries, cafés, boutique shops, and cultural spaces while preserving their original architectural elements.

Throughout the year, Monumental Callao hosts cultural events, live performances, art exhibitions, and creative workshops, making the neighborhood an active cultural hub that continuously evolves with new artistic projects.

Cultural and Urban Importance

Monumental Callao represents one of Peru’s most important examples of culture-driven urban regeneration. Beyond its artistic value, the project has contributed to improving the visibility of the historic center, attracting tourism, supporting local businesses, and creating new opportunities for artists and entrepreneurs.

From a cultural perspective, it showcases contemporary Peruvian creativity while preserving the architectural and historical identity of Callao. Its galleries, museums, and public spaces promote artistic expression and encourage interaction between residents and visitors.

Within the urban landscape, Monumental Callao demonstrates how cultural investment can transform neglected historic neighborhoods into dynamic public spaces that celebrate local heritage, creativity, and community engagement.

📍 Informational Profile – Cantolao Beach

Introduction

Cantolao Beach is one of the most distinctive coastal locations in the Constitutional Province of Callao, Peru. Unlike Lima’s larger urban beaches, Cantolao is characterized by its close relationship with the local fishing tradition, maritime culture, and the Pacific Ocean. Its rocky coastline, small harbor atmosphere, and views of San Lorenzo Island create a unique setting where everyday coastal life coexists with recreational and sporting activities. From an audiovisual perspective, Cantolao offers an authentic maritime landscape that reflects the historical identity of Callao.

General Information and Location

Cantolao Beach is located in the district of La Punta, within the Constitutional Province of Callao, Peru, approximately 15 kilometers west of Lima's historic center.

The beach lies along the Punta coastline, facing the Pacific Ocean and offering direct views of San Lorenzo Island, El Frontón Island, and nearby marine ecosystems.

Cantolao is a small pebble beach commonly used for recreational activities, kayaking, sailing, paddleboarding, and other water sports due to its relatively calm waters compared to other parts of the Callao coastline.

Its surroundings include residential neighborhoods, the Cantolao Nautical Club, fishing areas, and scenic waterfront promenades that contribute to its peaceful maritime atmosphere.

History of the Place

The history of Cantolao Beach is closely connected to the maritime tradition of La Punta and the Port of Callao, one of the oldest and most important ports on the Pacific coast of South America.

For decades, the beach has served as a recreational area for local residents while maintaining strong links with fishing and nautical activities. The nearby Cantolao Nautical Club has contributed to the promotion of sailing and water sports among both amateurs and professional athletes.

Throughout the twentieth century, La Punta developed as one of Callao’s most prestigious residential areas, while Cantolao Beach became an important gathering place for families and visitors seeking a quieter coastal environment than the beaches of central Lima.

Today, Cantolao remains one of the most representative coastal spaces of Callao, combining recreation, sports, and maritime heritage.

Highlighted Curiosity

One of the most remarkable features of Cantolao Beach is its privileged view of San Lorenzo Island, Peru’s largest island, as well as El Frontón Island, both of which dominate the coastal landscape.

The beach is also recognized as one of the main starting points for sailing and rowing activities in Callao. The calm waters created by the natural protection of the bay make it particularly suitable for these sports.

During certain seasons, visitors can observe seabirds and marine wildlife in the surrounding waters, reinforcing the area's natural and ecological value.

Cultural and Urban Importance

Cantolao Beach plays an important role in preserving the maritime identity of Callao. It represents the close relationship between the local community and the Pacific Ocean, reflecting traditions associated with navigation, fishing, and coastal recreation.

From a cultural perspective, the beach contributes to the identity of La Punta, one of Peru’s most historic seaside districts, where maritime heritage remains visible in everyday life.

In urban terms, Cantolao provides valuable public waterfront space that balances residential development with environmental and recreational functions. Its combination of natural scenery and maritime infrastructure makes it an attractive location for photography, film productions, and outdoor cultural activities.

📍 Informational Profile – Agua Dulce Beach

Introduction

Agua Dulce Beach is one of Lima’s most traditional and visited coastal spaces. Located in the district of Chorrillos, this beach represents a direct connection between the city and the sea, where everyday life, leisure, and Lima’s popular identity converge. Unlike other more exclusive areas of the coastline, Agua Dulce stands out for its accessible character and strong social presence, becoming an authentic setting for portraying real urban dynamics. Its vibrant atmosphere, especially during the summer season, positions it as a relevant location from both a cultural and audiovisual perspective.

General Information and Location

Agua Dulce Beach is located in the district of Chorrillos, south of the city of Lima, within the Costa Verde beach circuit.

It is a public and free-access beach, widely visited by families, groups of friends, and local tourists. It is characterized by its wide stretch of sand, moderate waves, and easy access through public transportation and main roads.

It has basic infrastructure for visitors, such as restrooms, food areas, umbrella rentals, and spaces for recreational activities. During the summer, it becomes one of the most crowded spots on Lima’s coastline.

Its proximity to other beaches in Chorrillos and to the coastal boardwalk reinforces its integration within the city’s system of public spaces.

History of the Place

Agua Dulce Beach has a long tradition within Lima’s urban history. Since the mid-20th century, it has been one of the main summer destinations for Lima’s population, especially for popular and middle-class sectors.

During the 1960s and 1970s, the beach gained great popularity as a recreational space, consolidating itself as an emblematic point of the Chorrillos seaside area. Its development was linked to Lima’s urban expansion toward the south and to the need for accessible leisure spaces.

Over time, the beach has undergone several municipal interventions aimed at improving its infrastructure, cleanliness, and safety. These improvements have allowed it to remain one of the city’s main coastal spaces.

Despite urban changes, Agua Dulce has preserved its essence as a massive social meeting place, maintaining its popular identity.

Highlighted Curiosity

A distinctive feature of Agua Dulce Beach is its name, which contrasts with its marine nature. The term “Agua Dulce” does not refer to the composition of the water, but to a historical name whose origin is not fully documented, generating different local interpretations.

In addition, the beach is known for its high concentration of visitors during the summer, gathering thousands of people in a single day. This level of attendance creates a very particular visual environment, full of color, movement, and constant activity.

Another relevant aspect is the presence of traditional street vendors, who are part of the cultural experience of the place, offering typical products such as seafood, drinks, and snacks.

Cultural and Urban Importance

Agua Dulce Beach plays a fundamental role as an accessible recreational space in Lima. Its public character and massive use make it a key point for social interaction, especially during the summer season.

From a cultural perspective, it represents an authentic expression of everyday life in Lima, far from more tourist-oriented or exclusive environments. This factor makes it a relevant setting for urban studies and audiovisual representations that seek to show social reality.

In urban terms, it forms part of the Costa Verde beach system, contributing to the offer of open spaces in a densely populated city.

📍 Informational Profile – Bridge of Sighs

Introduction

The Bridge of Sighs is one of Lima’s most emblematic and symbolic spaces, located in the traditional district of Barranco. This place does not stand out because of its urban scale, but because of its strong cultural, artistic, and emotional significance. Historically associated with romance, bohemian life, and artistic expression, the bridge has become an identity landmark of the city. Its aesthetics, history, and atmosphere position it as a highly valued location within Peruvian audiovisual storytelling.

General Information and Location

The Bridge of Sighs is located in the district of Barranco, in the city of Lima.

It was inaugurated in 1876 and is built mainly of wood, with an approximate length of 44 meters. It connects Ayacucho Street with the Bajada de Baños, a traditional path that leads toward the sea.

The bridge’s surroundings include narrow streets, old houses, art galleries, and cultural spaces, reinforcing its bohemian character.

It is considered one of Barranco’s main tourist attractions and a frequent stop for national and international visitors.

History of the Place

The Bridge of Sighs was built in the 19th century as part of Barranco’s urban infrastructure, when the district was beginning to consolidate itself as a seaside resort and residential area.

During the War of the Pacific, in 1881, the bridge was destroyed by Chilean troops and later rebuilt, becoming part of its historical memory.

Over time, the place acquired a deeper cultural meaning associated with the bohemian life of the district. Barranco became a meeting place for artists, writers, and musicians, and the bridge became a symbol of that creative environment.

In the 20th century, the bridge was immortalized in Peruvian criollo music, especially in compositions that link it with love, nostalgia, and Lima’s identity.

Highlighted Curiosity

There is a popular tradition associated with the Bridge of Sighs: it is said that if a person crosses the bridge for the first time without breathing and makes a wish, that wish will come true.

This belief has reinforced the romantic and symbolic character of the place, making it a common spot for couples and visitors who want to experience this tradition.

In addition, the bridge is closely linked to the song “Puente de los Suspiros” by Chabuca Granda, which contributed to its cultural diffusion and its position as an icon of Peruvian criollo music.

Cultural and Urban Importance

The Bridge of Sighs is one of Lima’s main cultural symbols. It represents Barranco’s bohemian identity and artistic tradition.

The area surrounding the bridge hosts cultural activities, musical performances, fairs, and events that reinforce its character as a creative space.

From an urban perspective, it is a transition point between the city and the sea, integrating historical, architectural, and scenic elements in a single space.

Its human scale and symbolic weight make it an especially attractive location for audiovisual productions seeking to convey emotion, nostalgia, or cultural identity.

📍 Informational Profile – Tarata Street

Introduction

Tarata Street is one of the most representative urban spaces of Lima’s contemporary transformation. Located in the district of Miraflores, this pedestrian street combines commercial activity, nightlife, and historical memory in a single environment. Its constant dynamism and accessibility make it a key transit point within the tourist and urban circuit. Beyond its commercial function, Tarata carries an important symbolic weight within Peru’s recent history, positioning it as a relevant location from both a cultural and audiovisual perspective.

General Information and Location

Tarata Street is located in the district of Miraflores, in the city of Lima.

It is mainly a pedestrian street, located near Larco Avenue, one of the district’s main arteries. Its immediate surroundings include restaurants, cafés, bars, hotels, and shops, generating a high flow of people throughout the day.

The street forms part of Miraflores’ tourist axis and connects easily with other key points such as Kennedy Park and the coastal boardwalk.

Today, it is considered one of the most active areas of the district, especially during nighttime hours.

History of the Place

Tarata Street gained historical relevance after a key event that occurred on July 16, 1992, known as the Tarata bombing. This attack was carried out by the terrorist group Shining Path during the period of internal conflict in Peru.

The attack consisted of the detonation of a car bomb that caused severe material damage in the area, affecting buildings, businesses, and homes, as well as causing human casualties. This event deeply marked the country’s collective memory.

After the attack, the area was rebuilt through a process that sought not only to restore the infrastructure, but also to revitalize the urban space. The recovery of the street became a symbol of resilience and transformation.

Over time, Tarata went from being a space marked by violence to becoming one of the most dynamic and visited areas of Miraflores, reflecting Lima’s process of urban recovery.

Highlighted Curiosity

One of the particular features of Tarata Street is the contrast between its historical past and its urban present. Although it was the site of one of the most shocking attacks in Lima, today it is associated with social life, tourism, and entertainment.

This contrast gives it significant symbolic value, as it represents the city’s capacity to rebuild itself and give new meaning to its spaces.

In addition, many visitors walk through the street without knowing its history, which reinforces the importance of its memory within the urban context.

Cultural and Urban Importance

Tarata Street is a clear example of how an urban space can evolve and adapt to new social dynamics. Today, it functions as a commercial and tourist axis within Miraflores, contributing to the local economy.

From a cultural perspective, its history makes it a reference point within the narrative of Peru’s internal conflict. This gives it an additional dimension beyond its everyday use.

In urban terms, its pedestrian character and integration with other areas of the district position it as a key space within the system of mobility and commercial activity.

Its dynamic atmosphere, with a constant flow of people, makes it visually attractive for audiovisual productions.

📍 Informational Profile – Jirón de la Unión

Introduction

Jirón de la Unión is one of Lima’s most emblematic streets, a pedestrian axis that brings together the history, commerce, and urban life of the Peruvian capital. Along its route, this street connects key spaces of the historic center and functions as a corridor where the traditional and the contemporary converge. Its intense activity, heritage value, and accessible character make it a representative location of Lima’s identity and a recurring setting for audiovisual narratives.

General Information and Location

Jirón de la Unión is located in the historic center of Lima, within the district of Cercado de Lima.

It extends for approximately 1.2 kilometers, connecting the Plaza Mayor with Plaza San Martín, two of the most important public spaces in the city.

Today, much of the street is pedestrianized, facilitating the daily movement of thousands of people. Along the street, there are shops, restaurants, galleries, banks, and historic buildings.

Its strategic location makes it one of Lima’s main commercial and tourist axes.

History of the Place

The origin of Jirón de la Unión dates back to the colonial period, when it formed part of Lima’s original urban layout. For centuries, it was one of the city’s main streets, housing residences of influential families, businesses, and social meeting spaces.

In the 19th and early 20th centuries, the street reached its greatest splendor, consolidating itself as the center of Lima’s social life. It was a place where the elite gathered, strolled, and participated in cultural activities.

With the growth of the city and the displacement of commercial centers toward other districts, the street underwent a process of change. However, in recent decades it has been the subject of urban interventions aimed at its recovery, including pedestrianization and the restoration of historic buildings.

Today, Jirón de la Unión maintains its relevance as a commercial axis, although with a more popular and diverse dynamic.

Highlighted Curiosity

One of the particular features of Jirón de la Unión is its social evolution over time. It went from being an exclusive space for Lima’s elite to becoming a place accessible to all sectors of the population.

In addition, along its route there are historic buildings with traditional Lima balconies, reflecting colonial and republican architecture.

The street has also been the setting for multiple historical and cultural events, reinforcing its importance within the city’s collective memory.

Cultural and Urban Importance

Jirón de la Unión is a fundamental axis within Lima’s historic center. Its function as a pedestrian corridor facilitates mobility and access to various services and cultural spaces.

From a cultural perspective, it represents the evolution of the city and its social dynamics. It is a place where tourists, merchants, and citizens converge in everyday activities.

In urban terms, its pedestrianization has contributed to the recovery of the historic center, promoting a more sustainable use of public space.

Its high density of activity makes it a visually dynamic location for audiovisual productions.

📍 Informational Profile – Rímac River

Introduction

The Rímac River is one of the most important geographic elements in the history and development of Lima. It has not only served as a source of water for the city, but also as a natural axis that has shaped its urban expansion and territorial configuration. Its course crosses the Peruvian capital from the Andes to the Pacific Ocean, connecting natural landscapes with highly urbanized environments. From a cultural and audiovisual perspective, the Rímac represents both the origin of the city and the contrasts of its modern growth.

General Information and Location

The Rímac River runs through the city of Lima, crossing several districts from east to west until it flows into the Pacific Ocean in the Constitutional Province of Callao.

It is approximately 160 kilometers long and originates in the Andes, in the Lima region.

The river is the main source of drinking water for the city, supplying millions of inhabitants through treatment and distribution systems.

Along its urban course, the Rímac is bordered by infrastructure such as bridges, expressways, residential areas, and industrial zones.

History of the Place

The Rímac River has been fundamental since pre-Hispanic times, when local cultures developed irrigation systems that allowed the use of its waters for agriculture.

During the colonial period, the river was a key element in the founding of Lima in 1535 by the Spanish, as it guaranteed access to water and facilitated urban life.

Over the centuries, emblematic bridges were built over the river, such as the Stone Bridge, connecting different areas of the city.

With the accelerated urban growth of the 20th century, the river was progressively integrated into the city’s infrastructure, although it also began to face pollution problems due to industrial and domestic activity.

Today, the Rímac remains a strategic resource, although its environmental management is a constant challenge.

Highlighted Curiosity

The name “Rímac” comes from Quechua and means “the one who speaks” or “the talker.” This name refers to the sound the river produced in ancient times, when its flow was stronger and more natural.

In addition, during pre-Hispanic times, the river was associated with religious beliefs, as it was considered to have a sacred and communicative character.

Today, although its flow has been regulated and its surroundings urbanized, the river remains a central element in Lima’s identity.

Cultural and Urban Importance

The Rímac River is a structural axis within the city. It has influenced urban distribution, infrastructure construction, and Lima’s territorial organization.

From a cultural perspective, it represents the historical origin of the city and its evolution over time.

However, it also reveals urban challenges, such as pollution and the sustainable management of water resources.

📍 Informational Profile – El Frontón Island

Introduction

El Frontón Island is one of the most historically and symbolically charged spaces on the Peruvian coast. Located off the coast of Callao, this island stands out not only for its geography, but also for its past as a prison and as the setting of key events in Peru’s recent history. Its natural isolation, rocky landscape, and strong historical significance make it a location of high narrative value, especially for audiovisual productions dealing with social, political, or historical themes.

General Information and Location

El Frontón Island is located off the coast of the Constitutional Province of Callao, on the coastline of the city of Lima.

It forms part of the group of islands of Callao, along with San Lorenzo and Cavinzas.

It is a rocky island with no permanent population, characterized by its rugged relief and maritime surroundings.

Access to the island is restricted due to its historical condition and logistical limitations, which reinforce its isolated character.

The island contains the remains of the former prison infrastructure, currently in a state of abandonment.

History of the Place

El Frontón Island was used as a prison facility for much of the 20th century. The San Juan Bautista maximum-security prison operated there, housing inmates considered highly dangerous.

One of the most significant events associated with the island occurred in 1986, during the government of Alan García. That year, a riot took place involving inmates linked to the subversive group Shining Path.

The State’s response led to a military operation that resulted in the partial destruction of the prison and the death of numerous inmates, in an event that generated national and international controversy.

Since then, the island has been marked as a symbol of Peru’s internal conflict. After the prison was closed, the facilities were abandoned, consolidating its historical and memorial character.

Highlighted Curiosity

One of the most striking features of El Frontón Island is its condition as an abandoned space with visible infrastructure. The remains of the prison can still be observed, creating a particular atmosphere associated with isolation and the passage of time.

In addition, due to its location and restrictions, the island is not a conventional tourist destination, reinforcing its enigmatic character.

In the collective imagination, El Frontón is associated both with its prison function and with the historical events that occurred there, making it a place of memory.

Cultural and Urban Importance

El Frontón Island has significant value within Peru’s recent history. It represents a key episode of the internal conflict and the country’s political tensions.

From a cultural perspective, it is a space that invites reflection on issues such as human rights, historical memory, and political violence.

In urban terms, although it is not directly part of everyday life in Lima or Callao, its presence on the coastline contributes to the identity of the coastal environment.

📍 Informational Profile – Larco Avenue

Introduction

Larco Avenue is one of the most important commercial, urban, and tourist roads in the district of Miraflores, Lima. It crosses the central area of the district and connects Kennedy Park and the Miraflores roundabout with the coastal zone, the Malecón de la Reserva, and Larcomar.

More than a transportation route, Larco Avenue functions as an active urban corridor where commercial activity, tourism, gastronomy, accommodation, entertainment, and public life come together. Its constant movement and its connection with the Pacific coast make it one of the most recognizable avenues in modern Lima.

General Information and Location

Larco Avenue, officially named José A. Larco Avenue, is located in the district of Miraflores, Lima, Peru. It begins near the Óvalo de Miraflores and Kennedy Park and continues south toward Armendáriz Avenue, the Malecón de la Reserva, and Larcomar.

The avenue extends for approximately thirteen blocks and crosses some of the busiest commercial and tourist areas of Miraflores. Along its route, visitors can find hotels, restaurants, cafés, bookstores, banks, clothing stores, galleries, casinos, offices, and entertainment venues.

Important locations situated on or near the avenue include Kennedy Park, the Municipality of Miraflores, the Luis Miró Quesada Garland Exhibition Hall, Tarata Street, Benavides Avenue, several major hotels, and the Larcomar shopping center.

Due to its central position, the avenue is frequently used by residents, workers, visitors, and national and international tourists. It is also an important route for public transportation and pedestrian movement through Miraflores.

History of the Place

The history of Larco Avenue is connected to the first stages of Miraflores’ urban development. During the early twentieth century, Larco was already one of the district’s central streets, although it was narrower and had a very different appearance from the avenue seen today.

Historical descriptions present the old Larco Street as a winding and paved road used by pedestrians, mule-drawn transportation, small buses, and the limited number of automobiles circulating through Miraflores at the time.

In 1946, Larco Street was officially transformed into an avenue. The construction project took approximately fifteen months, and its inauguration was attended by Peruvian president Manuel Prado.

The avenue was named after José A. Larco, an Italian citizen who arrived in Peru in 1838 and developed an important commercial and civic career. He was the founder and first president of Banco Italiano, promoted the Italian Hospital of Lima, and served as mayor of Miraflores between 1891 and 1893.

During the second half of the twentieth century, Larco Avenue became one of Miraflores’ principal commercial and recreational areas. Restaurants, cinemas, cafés, hotels, stores, galleries, and nightlife venues gradually transformed it into a meeting place for several generations of Lima residents.

The later development of Larcomar at its southern end reinforced the avenue’s role as a direct connection between the traditional center of Miraflores and the city’s coastal tourist circuit.

Highlighted Curiosity

One of the main curiosities of Larco Avenue is the way its route connects two different images of Miraflores. At its northern end, visitors encounter Kennedy Park, the municipal buildings, and the traditional commercial center of the district. At its southern end, the avenue opens toward the cliffs, the Costa Verde, Larcomar, and the Pacific Ocean.

This means that a person can walk along the avenue from the active urban center of Miraflores to the coastal landscape in only a few blocks.

Another curiosity is that the avenue was originally a narrower and irregular street. Its conversion into a modern avenue in 1946 formed part of the urban transformation of Miraflores during the twentieth century.

The name “Larco” does not come directly from Larcomar. Both the avenue and the shopping center are connected to the Larco name, while “Larcomar” combines the reference to Larco Avenue with the Spanish word for sea, “mar.”

Cultural and Urban Importance

Larco Avenue has major urban importance because it functions as one of the principal organizing axes of Miraflores. It connects residential, administrative, commercial, tourist, and coastal areas within the district.

From a cultural perspective, the avenue is associated with the development of modern Miraflores and with the social life of Lima during the twentieth century. Its cafés, cinemas, shops, nightlife venues, and public spaces formed part of the experiences of different generations.

The avenue has also appeared in literary and audiovisual representations of Lima. Its movement, architecture, commercial activity, and proximity to significant locations such as Kennedy Park, Tarata Street, and Larcomar make it useful for productions that seek to portray an urban, tourist-oriented, and contemporary image of the Peruvian capital.

Today, Larco Avenue continues to function as a central route for tourists exploring Miraflores on foot. It provides direct access to restaurants, hotels, cultural spaces, parks, shopping areas, the boardwalk, and the Pacific coast.

📍 Informational Profile – El Olivar Forest

Introduction

El Olivar Forest is one of Lima’s most representative natural, historical, and urban spaces. Located in the district of San Isidro, it stands out for preserving centuries-old olive trees in the middle of a residential and business area. Its value is not only scenic: it also has historical, environmental, heritage, and cultural importance.

General Information and Location

El Olivar Forest is located in the district of San Isidro, Lima, Peru. It is considered one of the district’s main tourist attractions and an ecological lung within the city.

Its reference location is Los Incas Avenue, San Isidro. Within the space, the artificial lagoon, the rotunda, pedestrian paths, green areas, old olive trees, and resting areas stand out.

It was declared a National Monument in 1959, a Monumental Zone in 1998, and an Environmental Conservation Area in 2017.

History of the Place

The history of El Olivar Forest dates back to the viceregal period. According to historical sources, the first olive trees were brought from Seville, Spain, by Antonio de Rivera in the 16th century.

Only some of those first plants survived, but over time they gave rise to a large olive grove.

The forest formed part of the former estate of the Count of San Isidro. Before becoming an open urban park, this area was linked to agricultural lands irrigated by the Huatica canal, which was important for the development of San Isidro’s old lands.

With Lima’s urban growth, the olive grove’s surroundings were transformed. San Isidro consolidated itself first as a residential district and later as a business district, while the forest remained protected as a green space.

At the beginning of the 20th century, houses were built around and within the park’s surroundings, several of them with European architectural influence, especially Tudor style. This contributed to giving the place an elegant and heritage-oriented urban image.

Highlighted Curiosity

One of the best-known curiosities of El Olivar Forest is related to Saint Martin de Porres. Tradition says that the saint may have participated in the planting of olive trees in the old Limatambo area, and one of the forest’s heritage trees has been linked to this story.

Another important curiosity is that several of its olive trees are more than 350 or 400 years old. For this reason, the place is not only a park: it also functions as a living archive of Lima’s natural and urban history.

The forest is also an attractive point for birdwatching. Species such as the vermilion flycatcher, the Harris’s hawk, the saffron finch, the Peruvian meadowlark, and the violinist are found there.

Cultural and Urban Importance

El Olivar Forest has urban importance because it preserves a large green space within one of Lima’s densest and most modern districts. Its presence breaks up the landscape of buildings, avenues, and offices, offering an atmosphere of calm, shade, and nature.

From a cultural perspective, it represents the historical memory of San Isidro and Lima. Its old olive trees connect the present-day city with its viceregal, agricultural, and republican past.

It also has environmental value because it shelters tree species and urban fauna. According to the Municipality of San Isidro, at least 25 types of fauna live in this space, including birds, reptiles, mammals, and fish.

Due to its aesthetic, paths, lagoon, and ancient trees, El Olivar Forest is an attractive location for audiovisual productions. It can represent calm, memory, romance, Lima residential life, mystery, or contrast between nature and city.

📍 Informational Profile – Morro Solar

Introduction

Morro Solar is one of Lima’s most important historical, natural, scientific, and scenic locations. It is situated in the district of Chorrillos, beside the Pacific Ocean, and rises above the southern section of Lima’s coastline.

Its importance is not limited to its panoramic views. Morro Solar is also a historic battlefield, an archaeological area, a geological reserve, a religious destination, and a place associated with astronomy and the urban memory of Lima.

General Information and Location

Morro Solar is located in the district of Chorrillos, in the province and department of Lima, Peru. It is a large rocky formation overlooking the Bay of Chorrillos, the Costa Verde, the Pacific Ocean, and a significant part of the city.

The rocky massif reaches approximately 281 meters in height. Its elevated position makes it one of Lima’s principal natural viewpoints and allows visitors to observe the coastline, nearby beaches, the urban area of Chorrillos, and the islands located off the coast.

Several historical, scientific, and religious landmarks are located within its territory. These include the Monument to the Unknown Soldier, the monument to Miguel Iglesias, the Morro Solar Planetarium and Observatory, the Pope’s Cross, the Sanctuary of the Virgin of the Morro, and the Cristo del Pacífico.

The Morro Solar also contains pedestrian and vehicular routes, lookout points, archaeological sectors, natural slopes, cliffs, military memorials, and spaces used for civic and religious activities.

History of the Place

The history of Morro Solar began long before the development of modern Lima. On its slopes is the archaeological complex of Armatambo, an important urban settlement associated with the Ichma culture between approximately 900 and 1460 AD.

Armatambo was later occupied by the Incas and became an important administrative and ceremonial center connected to the pre-Hispanic road system and the coastal settlements of the Lima region.

Morro Solar is especially remembered as one of the principal settings of the Battle of San Juan and Chorrillos, fought in 1881 during the War of the Pacific. Peruvian defenders occupied its hills and slopes in an attempt to prevent the advance of the Chilean army toward Lima.

The battles fought in this area resulted in the deaths of many soldiers and civilians. For this reason, Morro Solar became a place of national remembrance associated with the defense of Lima and the destruction suffered by Chorrillos during the conflict.

The Monument to the Unknown Soldier was constructed to commemorate the combatants whose names were not recorded. Other monuments located in the area honor military leaders and defenders connected with the battle.

Morro Solar was declared an intangible zone in 1977 and was recognized as a Historic Monument in 1986. It has also been officially recognized as the Historic Battle Site of Morro Solar of Chorrillos and forms part of Peru’s Cultural Heritage.

During the twentieth century, the place acquired additional scientific and religious functions. The Morro Solar Planetarium was inaugurated on April 23, 1960, creating a space dedicated to astronomy, scientific education, archaeology, and the history of the battle.

Highlighted Curiosity

One of Morro Solar’s most interesting characteristics is its geological origin. The rocky formation is considered part of the remains of an ancient coastal mountain range and has a geological relationship with San Lorenzo Island and El Frontón Island.

Its rocks and slopes contain fossils and geological evidence from remote periods. Marine fossils found in the area demonstrate that parts of this landscape were formed in environments very different from the arid coastal landscape visible today.

Another curiosity is the variety of functions concentrated in the same place. Morro Solar simultaneously operates as a battlefield memorial, archaeological area, astronomical observation point, religious destination, urban viewpoint, and geological study site.

The Pope’s Cross was built using parts of electrical transmission towers that had been destroyed during Peru’s period of internal violence. It was erected in connection with the visit of Pope John Paul II and can be seen at night from different areas of Lima.

The Cristo del Pacífico, inaugurated in 2011, is another of the hill’s most visible landmarks. Its elevated location allows the monument to be observed from the Chorrillos coastline and other districts facing the Bay of Lima.

Cultural and Urban Importance

Morro Solar has great cultural importance because it preserves different stages of Peruvian history within the same territory. Its archaeological remains connect the location with pre-Hispanic Lima, while its military monuments preserve the memory of the War of the Pacific.

It also has scientific value because its geological formations, fossils, museum, planetarium, and observatory provide material for the study of astronomy, archaeology, paleontology, and the natural history of Lima’s coast.

From an urban perspective, Morro Solar is one of the most recognizable elements of Lima’s coastal landscape. Its large silhouette marks the southern end of the Bay of Lima and creates a visual connection between Chorrillos, the Costa Verde, the Pacific Ocean, and the rest of the capital.

The place also has symbolic and religious importance. Civic ceremonies, commemorations of the Battle of San Juan and Chorrillos, pilgrimages, processions, and Holy Week activities are held in different sectors of the hill.

Due to its cliffs, arid slopes, ocean views, monuments, informal settlements, paths, and panoramic views of Lima, Morro Solar is an attractive location for audiovisual productions. It can represent historical memory, social inequality, isolation, danger, marginal urban life, spirituality, or the contrast between the city and the coastal landscape.

📍 Informational Profile – Santa Cruz Residential Complex

Introduction

The Santa Cruz Residential Complex is one of Lima’s most representative examples of modern collective housing. Located in the district of San Isidro, it was designed during the 1960s as a residential development for middle-class families.

Its importance goes beyond its housing function. The complex represents a period in which public architecture attempted to combine apartments, gardens, pedestrian circulation, community spaces, and urban planning within a single residential area.

General Information and Location

The Santa Cruz Residential Complex is located in the district of San Isidro, Lima, Peru, near the border with Miraflores. Its main reference location is Avenida Belén, between blocks 2 and 5, close to Avenida General Córdova, Avenida Pérez Araníbar, and the Huaca Santa Cruz archaeological site.

The residential complex occupies approximately 64,200 square meters, with around 18,000 square meters of constructed area. It consists of multifamily residential blocks, single-family houses, gardens, plazas, pedestrian paths, internal streets, and community spaces.

The complex contains approximately 20 residential blocks and was originally developed with 446 duplex apartments. Many of the apartments were designed with an area of approximately 80 square meters.

Its location provides access to residential and commercial areas of San Isidro and Miraflores, as well as parks, schools, local businesses, public transportation, and the nearby coastal area.

History of the Place

The Santa Cruz Residential Complex was developed between 1964 and 1966, during the first government of President Fernando Belaúnde Terry. It formed part of the public housing policies implemented in Lima in response to the city’s rapid population growth and increasing demand for housing.

The project was promoted by the National Housing Institute and designed by architects Jacques Crousse and Luis Vásquez. It was conceived as a modern residential complex intended mainly for middle-class families.

Before the complex was constructed, the land had been associated with military installations and training grounds connected to the nearby Cuartel San Martín. Part of the surrounding territory had also been related to an old aviation field.

The Santa Cruz Residential Complex was officially inaugurated on June 26, 1966. Its construction introduced a different residential model to San Isidro, a district that had previously been characterized mainly by large individual houses.

The project included duplex apartments, multifamily blocks, single-family houses, pedestrian circulation, gardens, plazas, and recreational areas. Its design sought to create a neighborhood where residents could move through open spaces without depending entirely on streets used by vehicles.

Over time, the complex developed a strong community identity. Several families have lived there since its inauguration, while later generations have continued occupying the apartments and participating in neighborhood activities.

Highlighted Curiosity

One of the most distinctive characteristics of the Santa Cruz Residential Complex is the shape of its buildings. The project combines long linear blocks with cruciform or cross-shaped blocks, creating different courtyards, gardens, paths, and neighborhood spaces.

Some buildings contain conventional apartments on the ground floor and duplex residences on the upper levels. These homes are connected through elevated corridors, sometimes described as “aerial streets,” that provide access to the apartments.

Another interesting feature is the use of open ground floors in parts of the complex. Instead of completely occupying the lower level, some buildings allow pedestrians to move underneath or between the blocks, producing greater visual and spatial continuity.

The buildings were positioned so that the apartments could face gardens, internal spaces, or surrounding streets. This arrangement gave natural lighting, ventilation, and views of the complex’s green areas to many of the homes.

Despite being surrounded today by high-value residential areas and modern apartment buildings, the complex preserves much of the scale and spatial organization established during the 1960s.

Cultural and Urban Importance

The Santa Cruz Residential Complex has architectural importance because it represents the influence of the Modern Movement on Peruvian social housing. Its design prioritizes functionality, open space, natural lighting, ventilation, pedestrian circulation, and collective life.

From an urban perspective, the complex introduced multifamily housing into an area of San Isidro previously dominated by individual residences. It demonstrated how a large number of families could live within a planned environment while retaining gardens and recreational spaces.

Its plazas, courtyards, paths, and gardens encourage interaction between residents and contribute to the identity of the community. Local anniversaries, sports activities, fairs, cultural events, and neighborhood meetings continue to take place within the complex.

The Residential Complex has also been used for outdoor film screenings organized for residents and the general public. These activities reinforce its function as a community and cultural space rather than only a collection of housing blocks.

Due to its modernist architecture, elevated corridors, gardens, courtyards, exterior stairways, and preserved 1960s atmosphere, the complex is an attractive potential location for audiovisual productions. It can represent family life, community, urban memory, childhood, social housing, or middle-class Lima during the second half of the twentieth century.

📍 Informational Profile – Jorge Chávez International Airport

Introduction

Jorge Chávez International Airport is Peru’s principal air terminal and one of the country’s most important transportation infrastructures. Located in the Constitutional Province of Callao, next to the city of Lima, it serves as the main national and international gateway to Peru.

Its importance goes beyond air transportation. The airport represents travel, migration, tourism, commerce, technological modernization, and Peru’s connection with the rest of the world. Its terminals, runways, control towers, boarding areas, and constant movement of passengers and aircraft give it considerable visual and narrative value for audiovisual productions.

Since June 2025, all commercial flights have operated from a new passenger terminal developed as part of the largest airport expansion project in the country’s history. This infrastructure has transformed the airport into a modern complex designed to strengthen Lima’s role as a regional air hub.

General Information and Location

Jorge Chávez International Airport is located in the Constitutional Province of Callao, west of the historic center of Lima and close to the Pacific coast. Although it serves Lima, the airport is administratively located within Callao.

Its international identification code is LIM. The airport receives domestic flights connecting Lima with different regions of Peru, as well as international flights to destinations in South America, North America, Central America, the Caribbean, and Europe.

The airport complex occupies approximately 935 hectares. Its modern infrastructure includes a passenger terminal with five levels, two runways, a new air traffic control tower, aircraft platforms, boarding bridges, parking areas, commercial spaces, hotels, logistics facilities, and access roads.

The new passenger terminal is approximately three times larger than the former terminal. It was designed to progressively reach a capacity of up to 40 million passengers per year.

The airport is operated by Lima Airport Partners, commonly known as LAP. The company began operating the airport in 2001 under a concession granted by the Peruvian State.

History of the Place

Before Jorge Chávez International Airport was constructed, Lima’s principal airport was Limatambo Airport, located in the district of San Isidro. As Lima expanded during the twentieth century, Limatambo became surrounded by residential areas and could no longer respond adequately to the development of commercial aviation.

A new airport was therefore built in Callao and inaugurated in 1960 under the name Lima–Callao International Airport. Its modern passenger terminal was officially inaugurated during the middle of the 1960s and became one of the most representative examples of modern architecture and infrastructure in Peru at that time.

The airport was named after Jorge Chávez Dartnell, a Peruvian aviation pioneer born in Paris in 1887. On September 23, 1910, Chávez became the first aviator to fly across the Alps, travelling from Switzerland toward Italy in a Blériot XI monoplane.

During the second half of the twentieth century, the airport became Peru’s principal point of international arrival and departure. It received millions of travelers, as well as political leaders, artists, athletes, religious figures, and other internationally recognized visitors.

In 2001, Lima Airport Partners assumed responsibility for the airport’s operation and began a process of renovation and expansion. The original passenger terminal was modernized, while plans were developed for a much larger airport complex.

The expansion project included the construction of a second runway, a new control tower, new aircraft platforms, access roads, logistics areas, commercial facilities, and a completely new passenger terminal.

The new terminal was officially inaugurated on May 30, 2025. On June 1, all domestic and international commercial operations were transferred to the new infrastructure, while the former terminal ended its passenger operations after approximately six decades of service.

Highlighted Curiosity

One of the most interesting characteristics of Jorge Chávez International Airport is that its history can be divided into several architectural periods. The airport opened in 1960, its former modern terminal became operational during the 1960s, and a completely new terminal replaced it in 2025.

The old terminal was one of the principal symbols of modern Lima during the 1960s. Its large windows, observation areas, aircraft stairs, arrival halls, and exterior platforms frequently appeared in photographs, television programs, advertisements, news reports, and Peruvian films.

The former terminal was not immediately demolished after the transfer of passenger operations. According to the Ministry of Transport and Communications, the building is intended to be adapted for complementary logistics, administrative, and commercial services within the airport complex.

The current airport has two runways and a modern control tower. Its larger expansion project also includes the development of an “Airport City,” conceived as a commercial, hotel, logistics, and business area connected to airport activity.

Jorge Chávez Dartnell’s flight across the Alps is commemorated every September 23 in Peru as National Aviation Day. The phrase traditionally attributed to him, “Arriba, siempre arriba,” is also used as the motto of the Peruvian Air Force.

Cultural and Urban Importance

Jorge Chávez International Airport has major economic and strategic importance. It is the principal gateway for international visitors arriving in Peru and the central connection point for domestic travel between Lima and the country’s different regions.

Its operations contribute directly to tourism, foreign trade, air cargo, employment, business activity, and the development of services associated with transportation and hospitality.

From a social and cultural perspective, the airport is associated with departures, reunions, migration, family separation, professional opportunities, diplomatic visits, tourism, and personal transformation. For many travelers, it is their first or last visual experience of Lima.

In urban terms, the expansion of the airport has influenced the development of Callao through new roads, hotels, logistics facilities, commercial areas, warehouses, transportation services, and infrastructure projects.

For audiovisual productions, the airport can represent international travel, diplomatic missions, escape, migration, emotional farewells, new beginnings, political intrigue, or the arrival of characters from abroad.

Its historical value is also important. Images recorded in the former terminal preserve the architecture, vehicles, aircraft, clothing, technology, and passenger routines of Lima during the second half of the twentieth century. The Peruvian film El embajador y yo includes sequences filmed at Jorge Chávez International Airport and presents the terminal as part of the modern image of Lima during the 1960s.
